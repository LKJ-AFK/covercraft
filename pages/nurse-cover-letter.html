Nurse Cover Letter Examples (2026)

Nursing managers hire for clinical judgment and reliability. This letter shows how to prove both — a measurable patient outcome, a routine you can be trusted with, and a first-weeks plan.

Full example · copy & customize Dear Hiring Manager, On the med-surg unit at St. Mary's Hospital, I managed an average of six patients per shift and cut our unit's fall rate by 30% over six months by introducing hourly rounding checks with a shared sign-off board. The board also made handoffs more reliable — nurses on the next shift could see at a glance which patients were due for a check. The staff nurse role at your hospital needs that same mix of clinical skill and teamwork. I hold my BSN, am trained in telemetry and IV therapy, and have precepted four new graduates through their first months on the floor. My charting is consistently current, which mattered on a busy unit where a late note can delay the next nurse's plan. In my first two weeks on your unit, I would complete orientation with your charge nurses, learn your fall-prevention and handoff protocols, and ask for my patient assignment to be reviewed with me each morning until you are confident in my workflow. Sincerely, Alex Morgan
Replace every number with your own real results before sending — recruiters check. Never copy facts you can't back up.

Why this letter works

  • A patient outcome, measured. "Fall rate down 30% in six months" is clinical proof — exactly what a unit manager screens for. — Open with a patient-safety result.
  • It shows reliability. Current charting, precepting four new grads, telemetry and IV training — trust is built from these specifics. — List credentials with evidence of use.
  • It respects the unit's systems. "Learn your protocols before changing anything" signals you will fit in, not steamroll. — Name the unit's processes as something you will learn.
  • A humble first two weeks. "Reviewed with me each morning until you are confident" asks for oversight, which managers reward. — Ask for supervision as a strength, not a need.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a nurse cover letter be?

170-200 words, three paragraphs. Nurse managers scan for safety, reliability, and fit — one outcome and one proof of each.

Should I list every certification?

Only the ones the unit uses. A pile of unrelated certs reads as noise; telemetry and IV therapy targeted to the job are signals.

What if I am a new graduate?

Lead with clinical hours: the unit, the patient load, one outcome you contributed to, and your NCLEX status. Preceptors value honesty about what you still need to learn.
